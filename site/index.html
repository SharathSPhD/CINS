CINS

Inverse airfoil design without an optimizer — geometry recovered from a target pressure distribution as one square Newton root-find, with CST coefficients as first-class unknowns in the flow solver's global system.

What is this?

Technical: Airfoil geometry parameterized by the Class-Shape Transformation is linear in its coefficients A. Appending A to a Newton-coupled viscous/inviscid solver's global system — with target-pressure rows and linear geometric-constraint rows closing the DOF count (M + K = nA + 1) — turns inverse design into a determined root-find: ~5–15 Newton iterations instead of 10²–10³ optimizer-driven flow solves.

Plain terms: Designers usually find a wing shape by trial and error — a computer guesses a shape, simulates the airflow, scores it, and repeats hundreds of times. CINS instead asks for the airflow you want and solves directly for the one shape that produces it, in a handful of steps, with built-in warnings when the wish is physically impossible.

The falsifiable test (T7)

Take a known airfoil, compute its pressure distribution, then hand only that distribution back to CINS. Success = the exact original shape re-emerges (‖A−A*‖∞ < 10⁻⁴) in single-digit Newton iterations with a quadratic convergence tail. Failure patterns map to four instrumented failure modes (rank deficiency, basis conditioning, leading-edge singularity, non-smooth transition closure).
