\section{Beta-function area-row derivation}
\label{app:area}

Section~\ref{sec:constraints} states the inscribed-area constraint row as a
closed-form Beta function without deriving it; this appendix supplies the
algebra and the two-surface assembly. The per-surface area under
Eq.~\eqref{eq:surfaces} is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{A}_{\text{surf}} = \int_0^1 \zeta(\psi)\,d\psi
  = \int_0^1 \Big[C(\psi)\sum_i A_i S_i(\psi) + \psi\,\zeta_T\Big]\,d\psi
  = \sum_i A_i \int_0^1 C(\psi)S_i(\psi)\,d\psi + \frac{\zeta_T}{2},
\end{equation}
using linearity of the integral, itself a consequence of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:surfaces}'s linearity in $A$, and $\int_0^1\psi\,d\psi=1/2$ for
the trailing-edge term. The remaining integral, per basis function
$S_i(\psi)=K_i\psi^i(1-\psi)^{n-i}$ with $K_i=\binom{n}{i}$ and
$C(\psi)=\psi^{N_1}(1-\psi)^{N_2}$, is
\begin{align}
  \int_0^1 C(\psi)S_i(\psi)\,d\psi
    &= K_i \int_0^1 \psi^{N_1}(1-\psi)^{N_2}\,\psi^i(1-\psi)^{n-i}\,d\psi \\
    &= K_i \int_0^1 \psi^{\,i+N_1}(1-\psi)^{\,n-i+N_2}\,d\psi.
\end{align}
The Euler integral of the first kind is, by definition,
$B(p,q) = \int_0^1 t^{p-1}(1-t)^{q-1}\,dt$ for $\Re(p),\Re(q)>0$. Matching
exponents, $p-1 = i+N_1$ so $p = i+N_1+1$, and $q-1 = n-i+N_2$ so
$q = n-i+N_2+1$, giving exactly Eq.~\eqref{eq:area-beta}:
\begin{equation}
  \int_0^1 C(\psi)S_i(\psi)\,d\psi = K_i\,B(i+N_1+1,\;n-i+N_2+1).
\end{equation}
For the default round-nose, sharp-tail class ($N_1=0.5$, $N_2=1.0$) this is
$K_i\,B(i+1.5,\,n-i+2)$, always well-defined since $i+1.5>0$ and $n-i+2>0$ for
every $i\in\{0,\dots,n\}$. No term in the sum is singular, despite the class
function's own $\sqrt\psi$ non-analyticity at $\psi=0$, which is
Section~\ref{sec:fm}'s point that the leading-edge singularity affects
flow-residual \emph{rows} and not CST's own geometric \emph{functionals}.

The enclosed area is the difference of the two surfaces,
$\int_0^1(\zeta_u-\zeta_l)\,d\psi$. Collecting one entry per coefficient
therefore gives a \emph{single} row $g$ whose upper block carries the
Beta-function values above, whose lower block carries the same values with the
opposite sign, and whose trailing-edge contribution is
$\tfrac12(\zeta_{T,u}-\zeta_{T,l})$. The sign on the lower block is not
cosmetic. Under the stored-sign convention of
Section~\ref{sec:cst-linearity}, lower-surface coefficients are already
negative, and applying the upper-surface form to them produces a row that is
silently wrong in exactly the way the trailing-edge row was once wrong.

The right-hand side $b$ is the target inscribed area, or, for the
self-consistency protocol of Section~\ref{sec:falsifiable}, the area of the
CST fit itself, $b=g\cdot A^\ast$. This target-consistent convention is
binding: the constraint right-hand side must always be built from what the
reference geometry \emph{actually has}, never from an idealised or rounded
value, or the constraint row itself introduces a spurious residual floor no
Newton iteration can remove.

\section{Degree-of-freedom accounting, worked}
\label{app:dof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:dof}, $M+K=n_{A,\text{free}}+n_\alpha$, is an assertion
rather than a derived quantity (Section~\ref{sec:newton-system}); this
appendix works it for the reference configuration and states the general rule
it specialises.

\paragraph{The reference case.} At $n=8$ per side there are 9 coefficients per
surface and 18 in total. The prescribed-leading-edge treatment removes 2 of
these from the free set: $A_{u,0}$ and $A_{l,0}$ are geometrically fixed to
the reference fit's own values rather than being Newton unknowns, leaving
$n_{A,\text{free}}=16$. Because they are prescribed directly rather than tied
by an equality row, no separate shared-leading-edge constraint row is needed
for this configuration, so $K=0$. The angle of attack is fixed for the
self-consistency protocol, removing the camber--incidence null direction of
Section~\ref{sec:ablations}, so $n_\alpha=0$. Equation~\eqref{eq:dof}
therefore requires $M = 16 + 0 - 0 = 16$ target-station equations, exactly the
QR-pivoted station count reported throughout
Sections~\ref{sec:falsifiable} and~\ref{sec:ablations}, and exactly the value
recorded in the run's own accounting record.

\paragraph{The general rule.} Restating Eq.~\eqref{eq:dof} in full generality:
for $n$ Bernstein coefficients per surface, hence $2(n+1)$ raw coefficients
across two surfaces, with $c_{LE}\in\{0,1\}$ constraint rows tying the
leading-edge coefficients, used only when the leading edge is left free rather
than prescribed; $p_{LE}\in\{0,2\}$ coefficients removed by prescription; and
any additional geometric constraint rows $K_{\text{extra}}$ from
trailing-edge thickness, inscribed area or curvature
(Section~\ref{sec:constraints}),
\begin{equation}
  n_{A,\text{free}} = 2(n+1) - p_{LE}, \qquad K = c_{LE} + K_{\text{extra}},
  \qquad M = n_{A,\text{free}} + n_\alpha - K,
\end{equation}
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:dof} rearranged, not a different statement. This is the
isolated-airfoil specialisation of the rigid-body bookkeeping quoted in
Section~\ref{sec:newton-system}: the three overall modes of scale, rotation
and translation are not exposed as free Newton unknowns in this
implementation, since chord, position and rotation are fixed by convention
rather than solved for, so they contribute neither to $n_{A,\text{free}}$ nor
to $M$.

The rule's lineage is Lighthill's own well-posedness
bookkeeping~\citep{lighthill1945new}: a conformal-mapping inverse solve is
well-posed only when the number of free shape parameters exactly matches the
number of independent closure conditions, contour closure, leading-edge radius
and circulation, that the target and geometry family jointly admit.
Equation~\eqref{eq:dof} is that same counting argument, discretised for a
finite-dimensional CST basis and asserted programmatically rather than checked
by hand.

\section{The onset-closure Jacobian rows}
\label{app:onset}

Section~\ref{sec:transition} describes the forced-transition onset closure
narratively; this appendix states its Jacobian-row structure explicitly, since
it is the one residual block in this paper's extended system that is not
simply reused unchanged from the host solver.

At a forced-transition onset station with endpoint states $U_1$ upstream,
still formally laminar-indexed, and $U_2$ downstream, freshly turbulent, the
vendor three-row station residual is
$R=[R_{\text{mom}},\,R_{\text{shape}},\,R_{\text{lag}}]$. Rows~0 and~1,
momentum and shape parameter, are the vendor's own ordinary turbulent closure
evaluated directly across $[x_1,x_2]$, an unchanged code path, so their state
and geometry Jacobian blocks are exactly the vendor's own, reused verbatim.
Row~2, the shear-lag equation, is replaced with the onset condition
$U_2[2] - c_{ttr}(U_2) = 0$, where $c_{ttr}$ is the same transition-correlation
shear-stress function the vendor's own transition update uses to initialise a
newly turbulent node.

Because $c_{ttr}$ is a function of the downstream state alone, specifically of
$\theta$, the kinematic shape parameter, the momentum-thickness Reynolds
number and the edge velocity at that node, its Jacobian row has the structure
\begin{equation}
  R_U[2,\,0{:}4] = 0 \quad(\text{no } U_1 \text{ dependence}), \qquad
  R_U[2,\,4{:}8] = e_{sa} - \left.\frac{\partial c_{ttr}}{\partial U_2}\right|_{U_2},
  \qquad R_x[2,\,:] = 0,
\end{equation}
where $e_{sa}=[0,0,1,0]$ selects the shear-lag component of $U_2$ and
$\partial c_{ttr}/\partial U_2$ is the vendor's own analytic derivative block,
already computed by vendor code for the identical purpose inside its
transition update: reused, not re-derived. The row's independence from $U_1$
and from the geometry is not an approximation. It follows structurally from
$c_{ttr}$'s own functional form, verified by direct inspection of the vendor
routine, and it is what makes this replacement row well-defined at a station
where the natural closure's internal transition-point solve has no solution.

\section{Convergence-order estimation and the residual floor}
\label{app:order}

Section~\ref{sec:h3} reports local convergence orders, and the estimates are
sensitive enough to the estimation rule that the rule belongs in the record.

Given a residual sequence $r_0, r_1, \dots$, the three-point local order is
\begin{equation}
  p_k \;=\; \frac{\log(r_k/r_{k-1})}{\log(r_{k-1}/r_{k-2})},
  \label{eq:order}
\end{equation}
which follows from assuming $r_k \approx C\,r_{k-1}^{\,p}$ and eliminating $C$
between two consecutive steps. It is exact only asymptotically and is
notoriously noisy on short sequences, which is why
Section~\ref{sec:h3} reports the spread as well as the median.

Three rules govern its application here. First, the estimator is evaluated on
the last three residuals lying strictly above $10^{-9}$. That threshold is the
finite-difference floor of Section~\ref{sec:derivatives}: with a step of
$10^{-6}$ and double precision, each Jacobian column carries a relative error
of order $10^{-10}$, so residuals at or below that magnitude reflect the
Jacobian's own noise rather than the iteration's progress, and an order
computed across them is meaningless. Runs reaching the floor in fewer than
three post-initial steps are reported as unscoreable rather than being given a
number, which is why seven of the twelve converged runs carry no entry in
Table~\ref{tab:h3}. Second, the raw convergence-order field stored with each
run does \emph{not} apply this exclusion and is never quoted; every order in
this paper is recomputed from the stored residual history under the rule
above. Third, the residual block the estimator is applied to is stated
explicitly, because it matters: for the ablation-matrix runs it is the full
extended residual $\norm{[R;\,T-Cp_{target};\,G\!A-b]}$, and for the
self-consistency run of Section~\ref{sec:falsifiable} it is the target block
alone, because the flow block is already converged at that run's starting
point and contributes only its own floor.

\section{Reproducibility and software environment}
\label{app:repro}

The repository at \url{https://github.com/SharathSPhD/CINS} carries the
engine, every configuration file, the archived run records, the figure
scripts, the test suite and the source of this manuscript. The flow solver
\pth{vendor/mfoil/mfoil.py}~\citep{fidkowski2022coupled} is vendored
unmodified under the MIT licence and is never edited; all solver access passes
through a single adapter module, which is what makes the forced-transition
closure of Section~\ref{sec:transition} implementable without a fork.

Every run writes an immutable record under \pth{experiments/results/} carrying
the commit identifier, a hash of the fully-resolved configuration, the random
seed, fixed at 42 throughout, and enough metadata to regenerate the run. No
number in this paper is hand-entered; every table entry traces to a stored
record, and Appendix~\ref{app:prov} names the record for each table and
figure.

Regeneration is by module. The ablation-matrix figures, being the convergence
overlay, the order sweep, the cost bar chart and the station-selection
comparison, come from \pth{cins.benchmarks.figures}. The geometry, pressure,
flow-solution and panel-gallery figures come from
\pth{cins.benchmarks.paper_figures}, and the formulation figures from
\pth{cins.benchmarks.paper_figures_theory}; both re-run the underlying
monolithic solves fresh rather than reading cached scalars, because the stored
records hold scalar summaries and not the coefficient vectors or field
distributions themselves. The cost, convergence and panel-outcome figures come
from \pth{cins.benchmarks.paper_figures_archive}, which reads only stored
records and re-runs nothing. Individual runs are reproduced through
\pth{experiments/run_t7.py} and \pth{cins.benchmarks.runner}, and the
test suite through \pth{pytest tests/}. The one qualification on the
data-availability statement is therefore that figures are regenerable from an
archived \emph{configuration} plus its recorded seed and commit, which for
four of the figure families means re-running the solve rather than replotting
stored arrays.

The environment is Python 3.11 or later with NumPy 1.26 or later, SciPy 1.11
or later, Matplotlib 3.8 or later and Pydantic 2.5 or later, as pinned in the
project metadata; the vendored solver is the 2023-06-28 release. All timings
quoted in Section~\ref{sec:cost} were measured on a single machine under a
single-threaded configuration and are reported for their ratio rather than
their absolute value, which is hardware-dependent.
