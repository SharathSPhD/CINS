\subsection{CST parameterisation and its linearity}
\label{sec:cst-linearity}

Let $\psi = x/c \in [0,1]$ and $\zeta = z/c$. The CST class function for a
round-nose, sharp-tail family is
\begin{equation}
  C_{N_1}^{N_2}(\psi) = \psi^{N_1}(1-\psi)^{N_2}, \qquad N_1 = 0.5,\; N_2 = 1.0,
\end{equation}
and the shape function is a Bernstein-polynomial expansion of order $n$ per
surface,
\begin{equation}
  S_i(\psi) = K_i\,\psi^i(1-\psi)^{n-i}, \quad K_i = \binom{n}{i}, \qquad
  S_{u}(\psi) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} A_{u,i} S_i(\psi), \quad
  S_{l}(\psi) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} A_{l,i} S_i(\psi).
\end{equation}
The upper and lower surfaces are the per-surface form of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cst-intro},
\begin{equation}
  \zeta_{u}(\psi) = C(\psi)\,S_u(\psi) + \psi\,\zeta_{T,u}, \qquad
  \zeta_{l}(\psi) = C(\psi)\,S_l(\psi) + \psi\,\zeta_{T,l},
  \label{eq:surfaces}
\end{equation}
with $\zeta_{T,u} = +t_{TE}/2c$ and $\zeta_{T,l} = -t_{TE}/2c$, both already
non-dimensionalised by chord along with $\zeta$ itself. The sign convention is
binding throughout: lower-surface coefficients are stored with their natural
negative sign, so the shared-leading-edge row reads $A_{u,0}+A_{l,0}=0$.

Figure~\ref{fig:cst-basis} shows the construction term by term. The class
function of panel~(a) is fixed geometry that carries no dependence on $A$ at
all; the Bernstein shape functions of panel~(b) are fixed polynomials; and the
surface of panel~(d) is the $A$-weighted sum of the terms in panel~(c). Only
the choice of $N_1$ and $N_2$ is nonlinear, and those are held fixed
throughout this work at the round-nosed, sharp-trailing-edge values
$N_1=\tfrac12$, $N_2=1$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_cst_basis.png}
\caption{The CST construction, evaluated rather than sketched: (a)~class
function; (b)~Bernstein shape functions at $n=8$; (c)~the weighted terms and
their sum for a fitted NACA 2412 upper surface; (d)~the resulting surface
against the section it was fitted to.}
\label{fig:cst-basis}
\end{figure}

The property this paper exploits is that Eq.~\eqref{eq:surfaces} is
\emph{linear} in $A$: the partial derivative
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \zeta}{\partial A_i} = C(\psi)\,S_i(\psi)
  \label{eq:dsurface-dA}
\end{equation}
does not depend on $A$. It is a fixed matrix, computed once from the chordwise
node distribution and cached. Recomputing it inside an iteration is the single
most common way to lose the performance argument, and it is unnecessary. This
design-independence is what makes the constraint block of
Section~\ref{sec:constraints} and the sensitivity matrix of the linear
pre-solve in Section~\ref{sec:presolve} constant operators assembled once
rather than geometry-dependent operators re-derived at every step. It does
\emph{not}, by itself, make the flow-residual block $\partial R/\partial A$
cheap; that block requires the flow solver's own geometric sensitivity, which
Section~\ref{sec:derivatives} shows is not available in complete form and must
be obtained by finite differences.

Two endpoint identities follow directly from Eq.~\eqref{eq:surfaces} and are
used as constraint rows in Section~\ref{sec:constraints}. At $N_2=1$ the class
function satisfies $C(1)=0$ and $C'(1)=-1$, so
$\zeta'(1) = \zeta_T - A_n$. This does \emph{not} take the same form on both
surfaces: with the boat-tail half-angle defined per surface as the wedge
closing angle, the upper surface descends toward the trailing edge
($\tan\beta_u = -\zeta_u'(1)$) while the lower surface rises
($\tan\beta_l = +\zeta_l'(1)$), so
\begin{equation}
  A_{u,0} = \sqrt{2R_{LE}/c}, \qquad A_{l,0} = -\sqrt{2R_{LE}/c}, \qquad
  A_{u,n} = \zeta_{T,u} + \tan\beta_u, \qquad
  A_{l,n} = \zeta_{T,l} - \tan\beta_l.
  \label{eq:endpoint}
\end{equation}
The sign flip on the lower surface is stated explicitly because applying the
upper-surface form to a lower-surface coefficient, which is negative under the
stored-sign convention, silently produces the wrong constraint row; that error
occurred once during development and is now pinned by a test against the
fitted trailing-edge slope.

The leading-edge identity has a short derivation worth stating, because the
curvature claim that follows from it is otherwise easy to misread. Near
$\psi\to0$ the class function gives $\zeta \approx A_0\sqrt{\psi}$, so in
physical coordinates $z \approx A_0\sqrt{xc}$, and comparing with the
osculating parabola $z^2 = 2R_{LE}x$ gives $R_{LE}/c = A_0^2/2$, the inverse
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpoint}'s first identity. Inverting the same expansion
gives $x = z^2/A_0^2 + O(z^4)$, so the limiting curvature at the nose is
$2/A_0^2$ and depends on $A_0$ alone; the next coefficient $A_1$ first enters
at $O(z^3)$ and therefore affects the curvature \emph{derivative}, not the
curvature. Curvature continuity across the leading edge, $G^2$ continuity,
consequently reduces to matching the leading coefficient magnitude on the two
surfaces, $|A_{u,0}| = |A_{l,0}|$. Under the stored-sign convention
($A_{l,i}<0$) the constraint row used to impose it is the stronger
same-magnitude, opposite-sign statement $A_{u,0} + A_{l,0} = 0$, which also
excludes the geometrically meaningless same-sign branch.

Figure~\ref{fig:cst-parameters} gives these identities their physical reading
and checks the design-independence claim numerically. Panels~(a) and~(b) plot
the two endpoint identities of Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpoint} with the fitted NACA
2412 values marked, so the coefficients that the inverse solve returns can be
read directly as a nose radius and a trailing-edge wedge angle rather than as
abstract weights. Panel~(c) is the one that matters for the Newton system: the
markers are the surface displacement measured by actually perturbing one
coefficient of the fitted section and re-evaluating the geometry, and the
lines are the analytic prediction $\delta A_i\,C S_i$. They agree to the level
printed on the panel, and the agreement does not depend on where the remaining
coefficients sit, which is exactly what permits $\partial\zeta/\partial A$ to
be assembled once and cached.

\subsection{Constraints as linear rows}
\label{sec:constraints}

Because Eq.~\eqref{eq:surfaces} is linear in $A$, every geometric functional
that is itself linear or integral in $\zeta$ becomes a linear row
$g\cdot A = b$ rather than a nonlinear predicate evaluated on a rebuilt mesh.
The leading-edge-radius and trailing-edge-wedge rows follow immediately from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpoint}. The one requiring a closed-form integral is inscribed
area: the per-surface contribution is
\begin{equation}
  \int_0^1 C(\psi)S_i(\psi)\,d\psi
    = K_i \int_0^1 \psi^{i+N_1}(1-\psi)^{n-i+N_2}\,d\psi
    = K_i\,B(i+N_1+1,\, n-i+N_2+1),
  \label{eq:area-beta}
\end{equation}
a Beta function, plus the trailing-edge term
$\int_0^1 \psi\,\zeta_T\,d\psi = \zeta_T/2$. Appendix~\ref{app:area} supplies
the derivation and the two-surface assembly, including the sign the lower
block carries under the stored-sign convention.
Equation~\eqref{eq:area-beta} is evaluated with a library Beta function,
never by numerical quadrature in a production constraint row; quadrature is
retained only as an independent check, which the implementation passes,
matching quadrature of the fitted NACA~2412 to $10^{-10}$. This closed-form
machinery is the same Beta-function apparatus \citet{joseph2021closed} use for
thin-airfoil lift and moment integrals against a Bernstein camber basis. This
is not a coincidence: both integrate Bernstein polynomials against the class
function, and it is the analytic kernel reused for the linear pre-solve of
Section~\ref{sec:presolve}.

Table~\ref{tab:constraint-comparison} sets these rows against what the same
constraints require under a B-spline control-point wrapper, where every one of
them becomes a nonlinear or non-smooth predicate evaluated on a rebuilt mesh.
That is the stack that forced surrogate-based search in the author's earlier
turbine-blading work~\citep{sathish2019sco2}, and the contrast is the reason
the present formulation can dispense with an outer loop at all.

\paragraph{Which rows the reported results actually exercise.} The constraint
machinery described here is broader than what the experiments in
Section~\ref{sec:results} use, and the difference should be stated rather than
left to be inferred. Every configuration reported in this paper runs either
with the leading edge prescribed, in which case $K=0$ and no constraint row is
assembled at all, or with the leading edge left free, in which case the single
shared-leading-edge row $A_{u,0}+A_{l,0}=0$ is used. The trailing-edge-wedge
and inscribed-area rows, including the Beta-function derivation of
Appendix~\ref{app:area}, are implemented and unit-tested against independent
quadrature but are not exercised by any result reported here. They are
presented as part of the formulation because they follow from the same
linearity and cost nothing to assemble, not as validated contributions to the
measured outcomes. Section~\ref{sec:limitations} records the consequence:
wherever the exactness of the constraint-row Jacobian is offered as an
advantage over the nested baseline, it is an advantage of the formulation and
not one this study has measured.

\subsection{The extended Newton system and DOF accounting}
\label{sec:newton-system}

The extended system appends the CST coefficients, and optionally the angle of
attack, directly to the flow solver's own global unknown vector. The unknowns
are the flow state $U$ of dimension $N_{tot}$, the CST coefficients $A$ of
dimension $n_A$, and the angle of attack $\alpha$, contributing $n_\alpha \in
\{0,1\}$ unknowns. The equations are the flow residual $R(U; x(A)) = 0$, of
which there are $N_{tot}$; the target-matching rows
$T(U) - Cp_{target} = 0$, of which there are $M$; and the geometric constraint
rows $G\cdot A - b = 0$, of which there are $K$.

Figure~\ref{fig:jacobian} shows the resulting bordered block structure, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:jacobian-blocks} states it algebraically with the dimensions
annotated for the reference configuration of
Section~\ref{sec:falsifiable}. There, \texttt{mfoil}'s global system carries
$N_{sys}=230$ stations, being 200 airfoil-surface nodes plus a 30-station
wake, with four unknowns at each: the three boundary-layer states
$\theta$, $\delta^*$ and the amplification or shear-lag variable, plus the
edge velocity $u_e$ that closes the viscous--inviscid coupling. Hence
$N_{tot}=4N_{sys}=920$. The prescribed-leading-edge treatment leaves
$n_{A,\text{free}}=16$ of the 18 CST coefficients as unknowns, and $M=16$,
$K=0$, $n_\alpha=0$. The assembled system is therefore
$J\in\mathbb{R}^{936\times936}$, and the recorded rank at the first iteration
is 936, that is, full:
\begin{equation}
  J = \left[
  \begin{array}{c|c|c}
    \displaystyle\underbrace{\partial R/\partial U}_{N_{tot}\times N_{tot}} &
    \displaystyle\underbrace{\partial R/\partial A}_{N_{tot}\times n_{A,\text{free}}} &
    \displaystyle\underbrace{\partial R/\partial \alpha}_{N_{tot}\times n_\alpha} \\[1.1em] \hline
    \displaystyle\underbrace{\partial T/\partial U}_{M\times N_{tot}} &
    \displaystyle\underbrace{0}_{M\times n_{A,\text{free}}} &
    \displaystyle\underbrace{0}_{M\times n_\alpha} \\[1.1em] \hline
    \displaystyle\underbrace{0}_{K\times N_{tot}} &
    \displaystyle\underbrace{\partial G/\partial A}_{K\times n_{A,\text{free}}} &
    \displaystyle\underbrace{0}_{K\times n_\alpha}
  \end{array}
  \right]
  \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{(N_{tot}+M+K)\times(N_{tot}+n_{A,\text{free}}+n_\alpha)},
  \label{eq:jacobian-blocks}
\end{equation}
square by Eq.~\eqref{eq:dof}'s construction-time assertion
($M+K=n_{A,\text{free}}+n_\alpha$), so the $M+K$ extra rows exactly balance the
$n_{A,\text{free}}+n_\alpha$ extra columns beyond the $N_{tot}\times N_{tot}$
flow block, which is itself always square.

Three of the blocks are inherited unchanged and one is new.
$\partial R/\partial U$ and $\partial R/\partial\alpha$ are \texttt{mfoil}'s
own analytic, sparsely-assembled blocks, reused verbatim.
$\partial T/\partial U$ maps the flow state to pressure coefficient at the
target stations: a selection matrix when a station coincides with a panel
node, and a two-node interpolation otherwise. $\partial G/\partial A = G$ is
constant and free, per Section~\ref{sec:constraints}. The new block is
$\partial R/\partial A$, which carries the architectural claim and whose
construction is the subject of Section~\ref{sec:derivatives}.

\paragraph{The zero block in the target rows is an approximation, not an
identity.} Under the original node-index addressing, in which each target row
read the pressure coefficient at a fixed panel index, $T$ depended on $A$ only
through $U$ and $\partial T/\partial A$ was structurally zero. Target stations
are now addressed by surface and chordwise fraction, and the pressure
coefficient at such a station is interpolated between the two bracketing
nodes, $T_k = (1-w)Cp_j + w\,Cp_{j+1}$. The weight $w$ and the bracketing pair
depend on node positions, which move with $A$, so
$\partial T/\partial A = (\partial w/\partial A)(Cp_{j+1}-Cp_j)$ is small but
not zero. The implementation neglects it. The neglect is defensible, because a
coefficient perturbation moves the surface in $z$ at a fixed chordwise grid
and therefore perturbs $w$ only weakly, and it is worth stating plainly because
it has a measurable consequence: the iteration is an inexact Newton method,
and Section~\ref{sec:h3} shows that the run using interpolated addressing
converges at a fast but roughly constant rate rather than with the quadratic
tail the node-indexed runs display. The same applies to the
$\partial T/\partial\alpha$ block, which the implementation likewise assembles
as zero on the grounds that pressure responds to incidence through the flow
state; it is shown as a zero block in Eq.~\eqref{eq:jacobian-blocks} for that
reason.

\paragraph{Degree-of-freedom accounting.} The original bookkeeping for this
architecture counted rigid-body freedoms explicitly,
\[
  \mathrm{DOF} = 2(n+1) - 1_{\text{shared LE}} - 1_{\text{TE fixed}}
  + 3_{\text{scale/rotate/translate}} + 1_{\text{stagger or }\alpha}
  \quad\stackrel{!}{=}\quad M + K .
\]
In the isolated-airfoil implementation reported here, the three rigid overall
modes are not exposed as free Newton unknowns, so the implemented squareness
condition is the simpler, equivalent statement actually asserted at
construction time and logged on every run:
\begin{equation}
  M + K = n_{A,\text{free}} + n_{\alpha},
  \label{eq:dof}
\end{equation}
where $n_{A,\text{free}}$ is the count of CST coefficients \emph{not}
eliminated by a prescribed-leading-edge treatment or by an equality-row
elimination, and $n_\alpha\in\{0,1\}$ depending on whether angle of attack is
a free unknown for the run in question. Appendix~\ref{app:dof} works this
accounting through for the reference configuration and states the general rule
it specialises. Violating Eq.~\eqref{eq:dof} raises at construction, before
any Newton step. This is the guard against the rank and degrees-of-freedom
failure mode of Section~\ref{sec:fm}, and it is exercised directly in the
ablation matrix (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}): deliberately over- and
under-squaring the baseline system, at $n=8$ per side with
$n_{A,\text{free}}=16$ and $n_\alpha=0$, produces the exact, actionable
diagnostics
\begin{quote}\footnotesize\ttfamily
"extended system not square: M+K=17 but\\
\hspace*{1em}n\_A\_free+n\_alpha=16 (M=17, K=0, n\_A\_free=16)"\\[2pt]
"extended system not square: M+K=15 but\\
\hspace*{1em}n\_A\_free+n\_alpha=16 (M=15, K=0, n\_A\_free=16)"
\end{quote}
with zero Newton iterations spent and no silent wrong answer. This matters
because a rank-deficient extended system does not announce itself: it presents
as Newton divergence, which is easily and wrongly attributed to the
leading-edge singularity instead. Instrumenting for it directly, rather than
inferring it from Newton behaviour, is the point of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dof} being
an assertion rather than a derived quantity.

\subsection{Derivative strategy}
\label{sec:derivatives}

The derivative-mode decision tree adopted for this work prefers, in order, the
analytic chain rule
$\partial R/\partial A = (\partial R/\partial x)(\partial x/\partial A)$ if
the host solver exposes $\partial R/\partial x$ analytically; complex-step
differentiation over $A$ if the residual-assembly path is free of absolute
value, minimum, maximum and comparison operators; and real central finite
differences over $A$ otherwise. Introspection of the host solver resolved both
questions empirically, and neither resolved in the analytic chain rule's
favour.

The analytic chain rule is incomplete for this solver class. \texttt{mfoil}
exposes a residual sensitivity with respect to the surface arc-length mapping,
but it is only a \emph{partial} geometric sensitivity: real and useful for the
arc-length coordinate, and in fact the route by which the solver carries its
own stagnation-point sensitivity, but missing the inviscid panel and
influence-coefficient coupling and the mass-influence operator through which a
geometry change reaches the boundary layer. Using this partial block as if it
were the complete $\partial R/\partial x$ would silently drop exactly the
terms the leading-edge failure mode of Section~\ref{sec:fm} is about.

Complex-step differentiation is blocked, not merely inconvenient. A bare
station-residual or transition-residual call, with fixed panels and a
complex-perturbed \emph{state}, is verified complex-step-safe and reproduces
the corresponding analytic state Jacobian to double-precision machine epsilon.
The \emph{full} augmented residual, which is what a perturbation in $A$
actually varies, since $A$ moves the panel geometry itself, calls the
panel-influence and stagnation-detection code paths. These use two-argument
arctangent, branch selection on real quantities, and a hard type guard against
complex node arrays. These are exactly the operators complex-step cannot
tolerate, and they sit in precisely the geometry-dependent block that the
analytic path was missing: complex-step cannot fill the gap the analytic chain
rule leaves.

The implemented strategy is therefore real central finite differences with
respect to $A$ only, never with respect to node coordinates $x$. This is a
deliberate architectural discipline, not a fallback of convenience. Over the
Bernstein orders swept here, $A$ carries between 8 and 24 free columns, so a
full central-difference block costs $O(n_A)$ residual \emph{evaluations} per
Newton step, not flow solves, against the alternative of finite-differencing
over $N_{panel}\gg n_A$ node coordinates, which would silently destroy the
cost argument Section~\ref{sec:cost} depends on. The resulting Jacobian
columns were cross-checked against the partial analytic block on the rows that
block covers, agreeing there to five or six significant figures, and against a
step-size sweep; no complete independent reference exists against which they
could be verified in full. The step size itself, $10^{-6}$, is not hand-tuned
per case: the sweep varied it across several decades and found the resulting
column agreement flat across the central range, with no visible
truncation-against-roundoff cliff at the scales exercised, so $10^{-6}$ sits
well inside a broad plateau rather than at a knife-edge optimum a different
problem instance would move. Section~\ref{sec:h3} returns to the price of this
choice, which is a residual floor near $10^{-10}$ that no Newton step can
descend below.

\label{sec:algorithms}

structural claims: everything left of the Newton loop is set up once and never
revisited, and the loop itself solves a single square system rather than
driving an outer optimiser. The two decision points are the only places the
procedure can refuse to proceed, and both are diagnostics rather than
tolerances: the squareness assertion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dof} is a statement
about degrees of freedom, and the realisability gate is a statement about
whether the target lies in the reachable set at all.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:monolithic} states the full monolithic iteration, target
generation through release-and-verify, as pseudocode, making explicit where
each of Sections~\ref{sec:newton-system} to~\ref{sec:presolve}'s mechanisms
sits inside the loop: finite-difference Jacobian assembly, joint
under-relaxation, the trust region on the coefficient step, and the
forced-transition closure. It is a direct rendering of the implementation, not
an idealisation of it. Algorithm~\ref{alg:qrpivot} states the QR-pivoted
station-selection procedure identified as the identifiability guard in
Section~\ref{sec:ablations}, reusing the pre-solve sensitivity matrix rather
than building a second one.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Monolithic CST--Newton inverse iteration}
\label{alg:monolithic}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require target airfoil (for $A^\ast$ generation) or externally supplied $Cp_{target}$; Bernstein order $n$; leading-edge treatment; transition mode
\State Fit CST to reference geometry $\rightarrow A^\ast$ (Section~\ref{sec:cst-linearity}); build $\partial\zeta/\partial A_i = C(\psi)S_i(\psi)$ once, cache (Eq.~\eqref{eq:dsurface-dA})
\State Generate target: direct-solve at $A^\ast$, with transition forced if the run so specifies (Section~\ref{sec:transition}) $\rightarrow Cp_{target}$
\State Perturb or randomise $A^\ast \rightarrow A_0$; assemble $G,b$ from Section~\ref{sec:constraints}
\If{initialisation is by pre-solve}
  \For{2 passes} \Comment{Section~\ref{sec:presolve}}
    \State Build sensitivity matrix $S$ at current $A_0$
    \State Solve the constrained system Eq.~\eqref{eq:kkt} for $A_0$; record realisability and model gap as two \emph{distinct} metrics
  \EndFor
\EndIf
\State Select $M$ target stations by QR pivoting (Algorithm~\ref{alg:qrpivot}), or evenly-spaced when ablating
\State \textbf{Assert} $M+K=n_{A,\text{free}}+n_\alpha$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:dof}) \Comment{raises before any Newton step if violated}
\State Direct-solve flow at $A_0$ \Comment{initial point for the extended Newton loop}
\State $k \gets 0$
\While{$\|R\|,\|T\|,\|G\| >$ tolerance and $k <$ iteration budget} \Comment{per-block residual norms recorded}
  \State Assemble $\partial R/\partial U$, $\partial R/\partial\alpha$ from the host solver (analytic, unchanged)
  \State Assemble $\partial R/\partial A$ by central finite differences over $A$ only (Section~\ref{sec:derivatives}), $O(n_A)$ residual evaluations
  \State Assemble $\partial T/\partial U$ (pressure-selection map), $\partial G/\partial A = G$ (constant)
  \State Solve the linearised system $J\,\delta \gets -[R;\,T-Cp_{target};\,G\cdot A-b]$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:jacobian-blocks})
  \State Compute joint under-relaxation $\omega\in(0,1]$: the smaller of the host solver's own state limiter and the coefficient trust region $\|\delta_A\|_\infty \leq$ trust radius
  \State $U \gets U + \omega\,\delta_U$; $A \gets A + \omega\,\delta_A$; $\alpha \gets \alpha + \omega\,\delta_\alpha$ (if free)
  \State Rebuild geometry from updated $A$; record diagnostics
  \State $k \gets k+1$
\EndWhile
\State \textbf{Release-and-verify} (Section~\ref{sec:transition}): un-force transition; direct-solve at the converged $A$; compare $c_l, c_d$ against the reference section's natural-transition values
\State \Return $A$, iteration count $k$, residual history, release-and-verify record
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{QR-pivoted target-station selection}
\label{alg:qrpivot}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require sensitivity matrix $S\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{cand}\times n_A}$ from the pre-solve (Section~\ref{sec:presolve}); free-coefficient index set $\mathcal{F}$; required station count $M_{req} = n_{A,\text{free}} - K + n_\alpha$
\State Restrict candidates to nodes at or aft of the prescribed-leading-edge fraction (Section~\ref{sec:le-identifiability})
\State Form the candidate submap $\tilde S \gets S[\text{candidates},\, \mathcal{F}]$
\State Column-pivoted QR of $\tilde S^\top$: $\tilde S^\top P = QR$, whose pivot ordering ranks candidate stations by decreasing residual norm in the orthogonal complement of those already chosen
\State Select the first $M_{req}$ pivoted candidate indices $\rightarrow$ target stations
\State \Return sorted station index set; report $\cond(\tilde S[\text{stations},:])$, a quantity \emph{distinct} from $\cond(J)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Forced-transition onset closure}
\label{sec:transition}

The $e^n$ transition closure used by \texttt{mfoil}, and by XFOIL before it,
is not smooth in the design variables. Within a station the internal solve for
the point at which the marched amplification factor crosses $n_{crit}$ is
smooth, but the surrounding bookkeeping is not: as geometry changes, the index
of the last laminar node relocates discretely, and that relocation
re-initialises the shear-lag state across the newly turbulent nodes through
the transition correlation. A coupled Newton iteration needs a continuous
residual; a jump of this kind stalls or chatters it. The mitigation is to fix,
or trip, transition during the inverse iterations and release it for a
verification solve afterward. \texttt{mfoil} exposes no forced-trip interface,
so this is implemented as an adapter-level replacement rather than a
vendor-code edit, in keeping with the discipline that the vendored solver is
never modified.

The first implementation attempt froze the laminar and turbulent node flags
and suppressed the solver's own transition update, but left the natural
transition-station equation untouched at the frozen boundary station. This was
\emph{insufficient}. Empirically, on NACA 2412 at $\alpha=2^\circ$ and
$\Rey=10^6$ with transition tripped at 5 percent chord on both surfaces, the
physics moved correctly under Newton pressure, with $c_d$ rising from
$0.00578$ to $0.00741$, but the solve never converged: the residual norm
stalled near $12.6$ for the full 50-iteration budget, because the frozen
station equation still solves internally for amplification equal to $n_{crit}$,
which has no solution once transition is pinned somewhere amplification is
nowhere near $n_{crit}$.

The corrected mechanism replaces the boundary-station residual with an
XFOIL-style turbulence-\emph{onset} closure. The momentum and shape-parameter
rows use the ordinary turbulent station closure directly across the whole
station, with no laminar sub-leg and no station-local transition point, and
the shear-lag row is replaced with the same onset condition the solver's own
transition update uses to initialise a newly-turbulent node, namely that the
shear-lag state equals the transition correlation evaluated at the downstream
node. Because that correlation is a function of the downstream state alone,
the replacement row has a particularly simple Jacobian structure, stated in
Appendix~\ref{app:onset}. With this closure the trip converges cleanly: at
5~percent chord the solver reports convergence with residual norm below
$10^{-10}$ and $c_d=0.01125$, strictly greater than the natural-transition
$c_d=0.005778$, as expected for the larger turbulent wetted area. A trip at
30~percent gives $c_d=0.00788$, between the 5~percent and natural values and
consistent with the expected decrease of drag as the trip moves aft, though
three points spanning two different residual closures do not by themselves
establish monotonicity. Releasing transition and re-solving from the converged
forced state reproduces the pinned natural baseline within its stated
tolerance, $c_l=0.449351\pm10^{-3}$ and $c_d=0.005778\pm2\x10^{-4}$. These
figures come from the closure's own verification run and differ from the
release-and-verify values quoted in Section~\ref{sec:falsifiable}
($c_l=0.449091$, $c_d=0.005774$) in the fourth and sixth decimal respectively;
that is a different run at a different paneling, not a restatement of the same
one. Rebuilding the global system after convergence with the replacement
closure still active reproduces a residual below $10^{-8}$: the converged
state is a genuine fixed point of the forced residual, not an artifact of the
step sequence. Every result reported in Section~\ref{sec:results} therefore
states its transition mode explicitly, and release-and-verify in
natural-transition direct mode is part of the protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:falsifiable}), not an optional extra.

Figure~\ref{fig:viscous} shows the state this closure operates on, and is the
reason a coupled solver was required rather than an inviscid one with a
boundary-layer post-process. The inverse system of
Section~\ref{sec:newton-system} is appended to \texttt{mfoil}'s \emph{viscous}
global system, so the six distributions in the figure are unknowns inside the
same Newton solve that recovers the geometry, not quantities computed
afterwards from a converged pressure field. The coupling itself is a mass
defect $m = u_e\delta^*$ entering the inviscid problem as a transpiration
source through the solver's mass-influence operator, rather than a literally
displaced body; panel~(f) plots the displacement surface $\zeta\pm\delta^*$ as
the conventional visualisation of that coupling. A geometry perturbation
reaches the boundary layer principally through the inviscid edge velocity and
the arc-length metric, and returns through the mass defect. The trip locations
are marked on each panel; the skin-friction rise at the trip in panel~(c) and
the corresponding drop in shape factor in panel~(d) are the closure described
above taking effect.

\subsection{Analytic pre-solve and realisability}
\label{sec:presolve}

Two jobs are done by a single linear pre-solve before any Newton iteration
runs, and the distinction between them is load-bearing for how results are
reported. Thin-airfoil theory is linear in camber slope and thickness, and CST
camber and thickness are linear in $A$, so the pressure response to a
coefficient increment is well approximated by a sensitivity matrix $S$ built
either analytically, from the Beta-function kernels of
\citet{joseph2021closed}, or, as implemented, numerically from
\texttt{mfoil}'s own linear-vorticity panel influence coefficients at fixed
geometry. Solving the constrained least-squares problem with the
Section~\ref{sec:constraints} rows as linear equality constraints,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix} S^\top S & G^\top \\ G & 0 \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix} \delta A \\ \lambda \end{bmatrix}
  = \begin{bmatrix} S^\top (Cp_{target}-Cp_0) \\ b \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:kkt}
\end{equation}
where $Cp_0$ is the pressure distribution at the current coefficient vector,
serves two purposes. It gives an initial guess placing Newton inside its basin
of attraction, which matters because the leading-edge region is exactly where
Newton wanders and the host solver's own documentation warns that the coupled
solve can fail from a poor initial guess. It also gives a
\emph{realisability} metric,
$\norm{S\,\delta A - (Cp_{target}-Cp_0)}/\norm{Cp_{target}}$, that flags
targets outside the CST-representable manifold \emph{before} a Newton solve is
attempted. The increment form matters: it is computable for a target whose
generating coefficients are unknown, which the difference against a notional
true $A^\ast$ would not be.

An adversarial review of the pre-solve found these two jobs' outputs had been
conflated into one number, and the resulting reporting convention is binding
for every number in this paper. First, CST-representability, the realisability
guard above, is answered with \emph{model-consistent} quantities: an inviscid
target against an inviscid linearisation. On the reference configuration this
is $3.90\x10^{-4}$, and the target is representable. Second, the \emph{model
gap}, how far the inviscid linearisation sits from the viscous physics the
Newton solve actually faces, is a separate, diagnostic-only quantity, $0.110$
on the same case. That is boundary-layer displacement effect, which no
inviscid model captures. It is \emph{not} target unrealisability; the
subsequent monolithic solve converging to $2.75\x10^{-11}$
(Section~\ref{sec:falsifiable}) confirms, for this self-generated target, that
the target was realisable despite the nonzero model gap.

Two reporting rules follow and are enforced throughout. Realisability numbers
are always labelled with the model they were computed under. And the
conditioning of the QR-selected station submap, approximately $90$ at the
reference configuration (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}), is never quoted as the
conditioning of the full extended Newton system. These are different matrices
answering different questions, and conflating them was one of the review's
confirmed findings.
