Four failure modes were identified before implementation as the ways the
architecture of Section~\ref{sec:formulation} can fail without that failure
being obviously attributable to its true cause. They are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:fm} with the diagnostic that detects each and the mitigation
adopted. The list is deliberately structured around \emph{discriminability}
rather than around severity: a coupled Newton solve that stalls looks the same
whatever caused it to stall, so the instrumentation exists so that a failed
solve reports which mode it hit rather than only that it failed.

Six per-iteration diagnostics support that discrimination, and
Table~\ref{tab:diagnostics} states what each is for. Two of them are not tied
to a single failure mode. The per-block residual norms discriminate
\emph{which} block of Eq.~\eqref{eq:jacobian-blocks} is stalling, which is the
first question to ask of any non-convergence, and the Newton convergence
history is the general-purpose instrument used throughout
Section~\ref{sec:results} and the basis of the convergence-rate analysis in
Section~\ref{sec:h3}. The remaining four map one-to-one onto the rows of
Table~\ref{tab:fm}.

One entry in Table~\ref{tab:fm} deserves expansion, because its mechanism is
routinely misdescribed and the correct description is what determines the
mitigation. The leading-edge failure mode targets \emph{rows}, not columns.
Section~\ref{sec:cst-linearity} showed that
$\partial\zeta/\partial A_i = C(\psi)S_i(\psi)$ is perfectly finite as
$\psi\to0$, tending to zero there, so the geometric columns are healthy. What
blows up is the flow residual's \emph{sensitivity} to geometry near the nose:
surface normals, metric terms and the stagnation-point mapping all have
unbounded derivatives there, so a handful of rows near the stagnation point
acquire enormous magnitude and destroy the coupled matrix's condition number.
This is a concrete instance of the point made in
Section~\ref{sec:cst-linearity}: CST's linearity keeps the columns exact, but
does not by itself guarantee the rows are well-conditioned near a genuinely
non-analytic geometric feature. It is also why the mitigation is geometric,
prescribing the nose rather than reweighting the basis, and why prescribing
the nose in turn forces the station-restriction rule of
Section~\ref{sec:le-identifiability}: the two are a matched pair, and applying
either alone reintroduces the degeneracy from the opposite direction.

All four modes were predicted before implementation, and three of the four
were subsequently observed in the runs reported here: the rank mode in the
deliberately mis-squared configurations of Section~\ref{sec:ablations}, the
basis-conditioning mode in the order sweep of Section~\ref{sec:nsweep}, and
the transition-closure mode in the stalled first implementation described in
Section~\ref{sec:transition}. The leading-edge mode is the exception. Its
mitigation was adopted from the outset, so it is inferred from the
station-restriction experiment of Section~\ref{sec:le-identifiability} rather
than observed directly in an unmitigated run; the row-norm diagnostic that
would show it is recorded but not presented here.
